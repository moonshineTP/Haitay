Haitay Wins on Service

Prior art, domain model, class positioning and project plan for the Haitay speedcubing timer (HUST IT4735 midterm, team of two). Research date 2026-10-08.

Bottom line. Haitay's device is reproducible prior art, so its standing in class comes from the service layer and from measured claims. ESP32 touch-pad timers with BLE have existed since 2020 (ojdip.net), GAN, QiYi and MoYu sell BLE timers with apps, and csTimer, Cubeast and a new Speedsolving.com timer (August 2026) already chart Ao5 to Ao100. No searched source shows the full combination of a standalone two-pad timer, store-and-forward sync with acknowledgement, and a persistent PC time-series service; this is the defensible gap, and it rests on search results only. Cuber pain supports the service, since csTimer users report losing thousands of solves and fighting the audio-jack path. Course rubrics reward a working end-to-end demo, a written spec and measured outcomes more than novelty, so the recommended scope pairs a demo (with a visible MQTT or REST hop) with a validation chapter. At the 8-week baseline the plan holds 121 person-hours of expected effort (plan figure 158 h against 160 h capacity at 10 h/person/week), with 6- and 10-week ladders that drop features in a fixed order. Four requirement defects need fixing before build: the 15 V power line, the 0.001 s latency line, the Ao100 location conflict, and the missing numeric acceptance criteria. Haitay is a practice timer because the WCA allows only Approved Timer List timers, and the HUST rubric is unpublished, so the lecturer must confirm team size, weight and demo format.

Prior art and establishment

Every Haitay feature exists somewhere, but the combination was absent from all searched sources. Hardware timers dominate on price and rules, software dominates on analytics, and hobby builds cover the electronics. Novelty therefore sits in the integrated pipeline and its validation.

Hardware timers

Stackmat is the de facto standard. Gen 1 debuted at World Championship 2003, the WCA adopted Stackmats in 2004, and Gen 2 left the approved list on 1 Feb 2023 (Speedsolving wiki; WCA post). The G5 (2021) adds 2-pad and 4-pad modes, 0.2 s hold-to-activate buttons and a 0.5 s lock after each stop. Its output is a reverse-engineered 1200-baud 8N1 serial packet on a 2.5 mm jack: state character, time digits and a checksum, roughly every 0.15 s (PaddyCo Protocol.md; ledmat). Gen 4 uses a 10-byte packet (m9aertner). The line-ending order is unresolved, and Speed Stacks publishes no protocol spec. The timer keeps only best stacking times, with no wireless link and no app (Speed Stacks G5).

WCA constraints

Haitay cannot compete officially. Regulation 7f1a requires "an approved timer from the Approved Timer List", which secondary sources reduce to Speed Stacks G3, G4 and G5; the list PDF itself was unreadable (WCA Regulations; Speedcubers Digest). Puzzles with electronic components are banned (3a4), so smart cubes are practice-only. From 2 Jan 2027 official attempts need a G5 in 4-pad mode (WCA post). Times under 10 minutes are truncated to hundredths (9f1), so competition resolution is 10 ms. No certification path for third-party timers was found. The inspection rules (15 s, +2 at 15 s, DNF at 17 s, premature stop under 0.06 s) are the reference behaviour for any inspection mode.

Operational pain behind the service claim

Data loss is documented: one csTimer user lost a log of almost 12,000 solves and another wrote "This has happened to me 5 times" (thread); another thread reports losses of about 4,000 solves (thread). Stackmat-to-csTimer setups fail on mic gain, noisy cables and combo jacks (thread). Cost complaints persist ("Stackmat timers are waaay to overpriced", thread). These support three service features: capture without a cable, backup that survives browser clearing, and per-user separation on a shared timer. Evidence for a shared-timer club workflow is indirect (school clubs, csTimer session-per-person workarounds).

Metric definitions and exact algorithm

The WCA defines only single, Mean of 3 and Average of 5 (best and worst removed, one DNF tolerated, two DNFs make a DNF). Ao12 and Ao100 are community conventions that trim ceil(5%) from each end (Speedsolving wiki: Average; WCA Regulations Art. 9). Label them "csTimer-style" in the report. The rule below derives from those sources and a third-party csTimer-style reference script (kana gist).

  1. Effective time: OK = t, +2 = t + 2000 ms, DNF = infinity. Store the penalty separately and compute the effective time once, since csTimer encodes +2 as an additive flag.
  2. Take the window of the latest n solves. Fewer than n solves gives undefined (show "-").
  3. Set cut = ceil(0.05 n): 1 for Ao5, 1 for Ao12, 5 for Ao100. Sort, then discard the cut smallest and cut largest.
  4. If an infinity remains, the result is DNF. Otherwise take the mean of the n - 2 cut survivors and round to centiseconds (integer rule: round(sum_ms / (10 k)), k = n - 2 cut).
  5. Current is the latest window. Best is the minimum over windows ending at each solve, with DNF windows excluded.

Edge cases: Ao5 and Ao12 tolerate one DNF, Ao100 tolerates five, and a DNF is trimmed as the slowest value. Example: 9.50, 10.20, DNF, 8.90, 9.80 gives cut 1, sorted 8.90, 9.50, 9.80, 10.20, DNF, survivors 9.50, 9.80, 10.20, mean 29,500 / 3 = 9,833 ms, shown as 9.83. Two DNFs in an Ao5 give DNF. Session mean excludes DNFs, and Mo3 with any DNF is DNF. Ties do not alter the sum. WCA computes from times already truncated to hundredths, whereas csTimer's display rounding is unverified; compute from stored milliseconds and document the choice.

Positioning for class standing

Peer IT4735 projects sit on a known archetype, and course rubrics elsewhere reward evidence over novelty. Haitay wins by combining a conventional pipeline with a real-time claim, an acknowledged-sync guarantee and measured results. The scope below keeps the Must set demonstrable in one sitting.

What peers build and what rubrics reward

The visible bar is an RFID kiosk: one ESP32, three readers, OLED, FastAPI with paho-mqtt and SQLite, and a React dashboard (IoT-IT4735 repo). The 20251 lab repo shows weekly REST, MQTT (HiveMQ), Kafka and ESP32 Wi-Fi provisioning work (lab repo). Comparable syllabi grade a proposal (5-25%), a midpoint (10-25%), a demo (30-50%) and a report (20-40%), with peer evaluation for individual accountability (Cornell ECE 4760; Berkeley ME 100; UVA). UVA names "techniques used for design and validation" and "measurable outcomes"; Cornell requires operation "according to a specification you write". Novelty alone is not a listed criterion in these syllabi.

HUST publishes little: IT4735 is 2(2-1-0-4) with a team exercise "building a specific application", and a 2021 lecture slide shows 50% process and 50% final (SoICT program; 2021 slides, [SNIPPET plus one fetch]). One credit equals 50 learner-hours, so the course totals about 100 hours per student (HUST regulation). The lecturer's profile leans toward edge AI (profile), so on-device statistics and trend analysis may be rewarded [INFERENCE].

Reference architecture and minimum demo

Chain: Haitay (BLE GATT peripheral) to a PC bridge (Web Bluetooth page, or Python bleak [UNVERIFIED]) to MQTT or REST to the ingest service to SQLite to the dashboard. The visible hop maps to the course's REST and MQTT labs, so the BLE design keeps a course-taught protocol on the data path. Keep the smart cube on the PC, because GAN needs AES plus MAC extraction and no ESP32 precedent exists.

Minimum end-to-end demo script (8 minutes):

  1. Power the device on USB-C for user A, then perform 12 real solves; show time, Ao5, Ao12; mark one DNF and delete one.
  2. Cut power during a write, reboot, and show the record count intact.
  3. Sync to the PC; drop BLE mid-transfer, reconnect, and show no duplicate or lost sequence numbers.
  4. Switch to user B; show the wipe happens after the PC acknowledgement.
  5. Open the dashboard on a seeded 3-month dataset plus today's solves; show rolling Ao5, Ao12, Ao100 and a PB event.
  6. Show the Bland-Altman plot of timing error with numbers.

Keep a 60-90 s backup video, a spare assembled board with identical firmware, and a dataset that works without a live device (demo guide [ANECDOTAL]).

Conclusion

The decisive variable is hours per person, not features. At the HUST nominal allowance of about 100 hours per student for the whole course, the 8-week baseline already consumes 79 project hours each, so the team should fix the horizon from real availability and let the drop ladder, not optimism, decide scope. The 6-week list works only if the sync layer moves to Wi-Fi MQTT, which also aligns with the taught stack.

Haitay's strongest argument against "csTimer already does this" is a failure demonstration. Pulling power mid-write and dropping the link mid-sync, with zero lost or duplicated records and a Bland-Altman plot beside them, shows engineering that browser timers do not need to face. Novelty claims stay modest: the gap is the integrated, acknowledged pipeline from a physical timer to a persistent per-user service, and it should be stated as "not found in searched sources".
